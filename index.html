About the project

Five minutes in someone else's life

Someone Else's Phone is a series of short interactive stories. Each one is a phone left behind by an ordinary person. You pick it up, look around, and slowly work out who they are.

Why phones

We walk past hundreds of people a day and decide who they are in about a second. The woman at the café window looks like she has nowhere to be. The neighbour at the bins is just the neighbour at the bins.

Their phones would tell you otherwise. The message they haven't answered. The search they typed at 2am. The voicemail they can't bring themselves to delete. A phone is the most honest record most of us keep, so that's where these stories live.

The hope is simple: spend a few minutes in a stranger's life, then look at the next one a little differently.

How to play

  1. Pick up a phone. Choose someone from the front page. Each phone opens on its lock screen, just as it was left.
  2. Use it like a real phone. Tap, swipe and scroll. Read the chats, open the photos, check the notes, the call log and the browser history.
  3. Pay attention. Messages and calls come in while you're holding it. Some things are locked, and the clue to open them is somewhere else on the phone.
  4. Fill in the picture. The amber dots at the top fill in as you find the moments that matter.
  5. Put the phone down when you're ready. You'll see who they really are and what you missed. You can always go back.

A note on privacy

Every person here is invented. These phones were written, not found. Please don't treat this as an invitation to go through a real person's phone.
